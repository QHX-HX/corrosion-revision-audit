# Revision cover letter skeleton

Address block, then a short letter that describes **this revision**, not the original
submission.

```text
[Author name]
[Group]
[Institution, postcode]
[City, Country]
Tel.: ...; Fax: ...
E-mail address: ...

Editor
[Journal]

Dear Editor,

We are pleased to resubmit our manuscript "[title]" (Manuscript ID: [ID]) for
consideration in [Journal]. We thank the editor and the reviewers for their careful
evaluation; their comments identified several substantive issues, and the manuscript
has been revised accordingly. A point-by-point response accompanies this letter.

The main revisions are as follows.

1. [Correction]. [What was wrong, what it affected, how it was fixed.]

2. [New data]. [Controls/experiments added and what they show.]

3. [Claims moderated]. [Which claim was downgraded and to what level.]

4. [Analysis reworked]. [Method change and how it is now reported.]

5. [Interpretation corrected]. [Assignment or model change.]

6. [Statistics and reproducibility]. [Test, replicates, procedures.]

7. [References checked]. [Duplicate removed, entries replaced, standard added.]

We believe these revisions address the concerns raised and that the manuscript is now
suitable for publication in [Journal]. The work is original, has not been published
previously and is not under consideration elsewhere. All authors have approved the
resubmission and declare no conflict of interest.

Thank you for your consideration.

Best regards,
[Name]
(on behalf of all authors)
```

## Rules

- Seven bullets is a good maximum; each one sentence or two.
- Describe the revision as submitted. Do **not** restate a claim the revision weakened,
  and do not claim novelty ("for the first time") that the reviewers contested.
- No duplicated sentences; the original submission letter often has copy-paste residue.
- Match the journal's tone: factual, no marketing adjectives.
